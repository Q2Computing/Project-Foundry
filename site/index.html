Q2 Computing / Project Foundry / Arbitrum Stylus

Prove your silicon in public. Keep the IP private

From foundry cell and RTL to GDSII signoff, proven on-chain and owned off it

Q2 Computing lets a fabless team prove its worth in public without giving the work away. Take a design from a foundry standard cell and RTL through to GDSII signoff; an independent oracle re-measures every claim from scratch on an open PDK, and the verified result is settled on Arbitrum Stylus. The proof is public and anyone can recompute it, while the netlist, the layout, and the innovation stay private. That split turns a private consultancy into a public commodity: the method and the scoreboard are open, so more people can learn and compete, while each proven improvement still earns. The same discipline carries from the open sky130 node to the open 3nm process, where the wins are worth real money, opening a class of American, foundry-grade design assets that institutional and retail capital can underwrite

Two objectives, one design space

Every point on both views is the same set of measured cells at 250 fF, read two ways. Performance asks who is fastest inside the foundry's own energy budget; a same-area dopant swap wins by up to 22% EDP. Energy asks who spends least over a real duty cycle; for anything that idles, which is most of what 130 nm serves, the foundry's low-leakage default wins by up to 160x. Neither answer is universal, so the agent optimizes for whichever the workload needs, a choice one fixed library cannot make. Toggle the view

Same finger count as buf_16, dopant masks swapped: 22% lower EDP inside the foundry's own energy band, from the standard-Vt pfet the leakage-tuned library never uses. Over-driven cells (more fingers) fall outside the band and are the wrong move. Reproducible with analysis/linearcomb.py

One rung up: the full adder

The swap is not a cell trick, it climbs. Applied to the foundry's own 1-bit full adder, the atom of arithmetic that every ALU and multiplier is built from, the same-area dopant swap keeps the identical 28-transistor mirror-adder topology, so the truth table is unchanged by construction. On the carry path it is faster and slightly leaner, at the same leakage tradeoff. We stop here, at the last rung where the improvement is still provable against the foundry part

Settlement on Arbitrum Stylus

The point of a win is not a screenshot, it is a durable, verifiable record. Each verified improvement is committed as a hash for a Stylus contract on Arbitrum to record. Anyone holding the netlist can recompute the hash and check it; no one else can read the design

record(bytes32 artifact_hash, uint16 label, uint16 context)
// best in-band win: EDP +22% vs buf_16, same area
record(0xbbe5a7f1...f8f7c, 2200, 16)
//         hash of the design bundle   basis points   drive strength

label is basis points of EDP improvement, context is the part's drive strength. The hash is the artifact; the netlist never has to be published. The contract is written in Rust for Arbitrum Stylus and passes cargo stylus check. Status: not deployed. anchor.py prepares this record() call, it does not submit it (that needs a funded key)

Why it holds up

Same transistors

Candidates use only the nfet_01v8 and pfet_01v8_hvt units the foundry buffers are built from. A win is a better arrangement, not a faster device

Independent judge

The generator never grades its own work. A clean CI runner fetches the pinned PDK and reproduces every number bit for bit

Disclosure safe

Only a hash and a coarse label reach the chain. Anyone with the netlist can verify, no one else can read it

Scope, stated plainly: these are deck-clean, SPICE-characterized results on the open PDK, not foundry-qualified cells. Area is a transistor-width proxy, not laid-out area. Layout, DRC and LVS are the next step
